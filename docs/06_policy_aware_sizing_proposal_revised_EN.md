# Revised PhD Proposal: When Does Perfect Foresight Mis-Size Campus Energy Assets?

**Proposed title:** Policy-Aware Sizing of Campus Photovoltaics and Battery Storage with Smart EV Charging  
**Researcher:** Thitipong Rotjanakorn · Faculty of Engineering, Khon Kaen University (KKU)  
**Status:** Revised working proposal for advisor review. This document governs the revised research design and comparator definitions; the previous Fast-Track Specification and Detailed Proposal remain background. No field access, thesis approval, site result, or intervention authorization is asserted.

## Abstract

A campus can choose PV and battery capacities using historical records as if their future values were known, but those assets will be operated by a controller that sees only measurements and forecasts available at decision time. The economically preferred capacity can change when the controller must carry a running billed peak across the month and cannot rely on actual future EV departures. This project will develop and test a **billing-state-aware, controller-in-the-loop sizing method** for a finite catalog of deployable designs. The method will use an optimistic dispatch bound to screen designs, a causal replay to rank them, and a crossed evaluation to separate the optimism in predicted operating value from the decision loss caused by selecting the wrong capacity. The primary evidence route pairs held-out billing replay with an authorized V1G charging pilot. If field control is unavailable, a separate, advisor-approved route requires validation across independent measured sites. Synthetic data are for method checks only.

## 1. What the thesis must establish

The thesis will test **when explicitly sizing for an implementable control policy changes a capital decision and improves out-of-sample cost at equivalent service**. The two proposed mechanisms are (i) remembering the billed peak and tariff history when deciding whether to preserve battery capacity for later in the month and (ii) reserving charging capacity using available commitments rather than using realized departures retrospectively. The proposed outputs are a method that produces a traceable sizing decision, a measured decomposition of the perfect-foresight error, and evidence identifying operating conditions where the extra modeling complexity does or does not pay.

Standard dispatch equations, the inequality between pooled and separate peaks, nested feasible-set comparisons, an EV feasibility LP, battery-cost sweeps, and solver implementation are supporting tools. None is presented as original knowledge. CVaR, pooled-meter comparisons, zero-BESS classifications, and Scope 2 reporting are secondary diagnostics, not independent thesis contributions. Demand charges appear in the problem formulation only for accounts where they are verified, not as a universal campus assumption or a premise embedded in the title.

## 2. Research questions and proposed decision rules

Numerical thresholds are **proposed design choices**, not site facts or expected effects. Confirm them with the advisor and site operator before opening holdout data. Register the final rules, comparison set, dataset exclusions and code version together. If a threshold must change, treat the prior analysis as exploratory and use fresh confirmation data.

| Question | Falsifiable claim and result that would not support it |
| --- | --- |
| **RQ1. Does sizing with the actual causal controller improve the capital decision?** | **H1:** Under the same locked controller and service constraints, controller-aware sizing reduces mean paired lifecycle cost by more than **3% of the common no-new-investment baseline lifecycle cost**, relative to a clairvoyantly selected design. Support requires the lower one-sided 95% uncertainty bound to exceed the threshold. If the upper bound is at or below it, reject the claimed material gain for the tested population; otherwise report inconclusive. An oracle-designed capacity that performs equally well or better is a meaningful result. |
| **RQ2. Which operational assumptions change the capacity recommendation?** | **H2:** Where demand charges and usable EV commitments are verified, the interaction between billing-peak memory and commitment protection changes the relative economic preference for a positive BESS design versus a zero-BESS alternative by more than **1% of common baseline lifecycle cost**. Use a held-out crossed comparison; if the uncertainty interval lies wholly within ±1%, conclude practical equivalence. If it crosses a boundary, report inconclusive. If commitments or demand charges do not exist, do not test that interaction as a local effect. |
| **RQ3. Does the controller reduce peak-window import without materially reducing charging service?** | **H3, field route:** In an approved pilot, mean paired change in the predeclared demand-window daily maximum improves by at least **5% of the paired reference mean**, and the upper one-sided 95% bound on the increase in eligible-session shortfall rate is below **2 percentage points**. Claim success only if both conditions are supported by their intervals and there is no unresolved safety event. Report the result as daily operational evidence, not billed monthly savings. |

For H2, select and lock the candidate positive storage design and the corresponding zero-BESS alternative on development data. For each, score the four combinations of peak-state input (on/off) and commitment reserve (on/off) using the same forecasts, tariff, hardware, common hard safety limits and observed charging information. The difference of the two designs' differences-in-differences defines the interaction; neither controller may use actual future departure. In the peak-memory-off arm, omit the observed running peak from the decision objective only; still calculate the full actual bill after replay. In the reserve-off arm retain electrical protection and use the same input requests but remove the explicit first-step recoverability check. A commitment-off arm that breaches service is evidence of a trade-off, not a cheaper feasible alternative. Do not run a deliberately degraded controller on live equipment.

## 4. Method: select assets for the controller that will actually operate them

### 4.1 Decision-time controller

Choose a single-stage catalog of admissible PV kWp and BESS kW/kWh configurations for each verified connection, including no-new-investment and zero BESS. Keep EV infrastructure fixed and use V1G only. Candidate feasibility uses documented roof, import and equipment limits. The base case has no export or cross-account netting. Select one policy family and calibrate its parameters on development data only. Use the same policy at every capacity unless a capacity-specific safety constraint requires a documented change.

At interval $t$, observe battery state of charge, completed demand windows, the running peak for each billed register, verified historical tariff state if applicable, active EV connections and requests already disclosed. Use a 24-hour rolling horizon sampled natively at the billing-compatible interval; execute one interval, update the state and solve again. Start with same-weekday seasonal persistence for non-EV load, earlier-day PV persistence or timestamped weather inputs, and residual forecasts calibrated by chronological development blocks. Archived forecasts are admissible only if their issue time precedes the decision. Actual next-day values, session outcomes and the end-of-month peak are never policy inputs.

For a simple linear demand-charge register, write the running peak as $q_t$ and the new horizon envelope as $z$. Use $z\ge q_t$ and $z\ge \widehat g_W$ for each eligible forecast window $W$, accounting for the observed part of a partially elapsed averaging window. Let $r_t$ be a development-only reference peak for the remaining billing period, using coarse calendar/season strata; keep it fixed with respect to the current decision. A candidate control policy charges the horizon increment above $b_t=\max(q_t,r_t)$ using $\pi^{D}\max(0,z-b_t)$ plus an immediate energy cost, a battery wear term and a terminal SOC value or reserve. Compare against the **peak-so-far-only** reference $r_t=0$ when applicable. This approximation guides operation, but final bills are calculated from realized peaks under the actual tariff, not from the controller's surrogate objective. If no demand charge applies, remove the billed-peak objective and re-evaluate whether the site can answer the main research question.

For a consenting session with an energy commitment and a declared time through which the driver expects to remain connected and by which the promised energy must be delivered, reserve a feasible schedule for its remaining energy using active-session charging limits and station capacity. For each proposed first dispatch, verify that those commitments remain jointly attainable under the declared connection windows. Use available current limits and confirmed assignments; preserve an operator-approved fallback if measurements or control fail. Unknown-retention sessions use the safe default, not estimated departure as a guarantee. Early disconnects, failures and sessions without a request are tracked separately; report all enrollment and exclusion rates. The feasibility commitment is conditional on agreed availability and hardware service, not a guarantee against unexpected departure.

### 4.2 Outer search and auditable certificate

For each design $x$ in the frozen catalog $\mathcal X$, evaluate a common present-value cost with capital cost, energy, demand and permitted lifecycle terms, using development trajectories $s$ with weights $p_s$. Let $F^{\rm or}_s(x)$ be the minimum operating cost under all realized trajectory information, and $F^\pi_s(x)$ the cost of the configured causal policy replay. Both use identical physical constraints and service definitions in the comparison. Define

$$
L(x)=K(x)+\sum_s p_s F^{\rm or}_s(x),\qquad
J_\pi(x)=K(x)+\sum_s p_s F^\pi_s(x).
$$

When the replay follows the same constraints, it lies in the oracle's feasible set, so $L(x)\le J_\pi(x)$. For a minimization oracle that has not converged, use its valid **lower bound**, never its best feasible incumbent. Evaluate a diverse starting subset causally, in ascending oracle lower-bound order within capacity strata; then process remaining designs in increasing lower-bound order. Exclude a candidate only if its certified lower bound exceeds the feasible causal incumbent $U$. An anytime lower bound is the minimum of the incumbent and the valid bounds for untested designs. Continue until the gap $U-\mathrm{LB}$ is at most **0.5% of development baseline lifecycle cost**, or report the outstanding gap. The comparator is exhaustive causal evaluation for small catalogs and a stratified search at equal replay budget for larger catalogs.

This establishes a bound for the **specified policy, finite catalog and development data**, not optimal continuous capacity, all possible policies, or future performance. The bound is an audit device based on feasible-set inclusion, not itself a claim of mathematical novelty. Evaluate whether the screening saves enough replay effort (proposed engineering target: at least 25% with the same tolerance) to justify using it. Failed causal solves use a documented feasible fallback where possible; service or physical failures cannot become zero-cost observations.

### 4.3 Separate value optimism from the cost of the chosen design

Lock $x_{\rm or}$ from the oracle sizing rule and $x_\pi$ from policy-aware sizing before holdout. On identical held-out trajectories, evaluate both designs under both dispatch settings with all costs on the same basis. Report:

1. **Operating optimism at fixed design:** $J_\pi^{H}(x)-J_{\rm or}^{H}(x)$ for each locked design. A negative value beyond solver tolerance flags an invalid comparator, not a benefit of limited information.
2. **Sizing loss under the deployed policy:** $J_\pi^{H}(x_{\rm or})-J_\pi^{H}(x_\pi)$. This can be negative on holdout even if the causal design wins on development data; H1 can fail.
3. **Capacity displacement:** paired differences in kWp, kW and kWh, including a separate check of designs constrained to zero BESS. A zero-BESS design counts as materially equivalent only if its extra cost is supported within a preregistered **1%** threshold inclusive of solver uncertainty. To attribute it to V1G, repeat the matched comparison without smart flexibility and verify the classification changes.

**Testable ranking diagnostic.** Let A be the cheaper design under oracle dispatch, and B an alternative. Define the oracle cost advantage of A as the oracle cost of B minus the oracle cost of A. Define each design's information gap as causal cost minus oracle cost on the same chronology. B overtakes A under causal operation precisely when A's information gap exceeds B's by more than A's oracle cost advantage. This follows by subtracting paired costs. The challenge is not proving this identity, but testing whether the peak-memory and commitment mechanisms predict *where* the crossover happens on data that were not used to fit them. As a secondary test, freeze a simple screening rule on development data using peak headroom, remaining time in the billing cycle, and share of charging energy under usable commitments. Compare its holdout crossover decisions with a simpler rule based on the oracle cost margin alone. Report errors and abstentions; do not treat an explanatory rule fitted to holdout outcomes as a validated predictor.

The retrospective oracle is explicitly a lower-cost bound for matched service and physics. It is not a forecast or evidence of realizable savings.

## 5. Validation and measured evidence

**Historical replay.** Freeze capacity catalog, costs, topology, rate interpretations, forecasting, calibration, response variables, thresholds and missing-data rules before the chronological holdout. Use scenario-specific session sets $J_{m,s}$, fixed or validated port assignment, energy balance and arrival/departure checks. Carry billing state and battery SOC across intervals and cycles; state at the start of holdout must be obtained without looking ahead. Use the same original observations for paired policy comparisons. Resample contiguous development blocks at 12, 24 and 48 sampled paired blocks (with replacement, where supported), using five recorded seeds at each size. Accept stability only if selected capacities differ by no more than one predeclared catalog step in at least four of five runs at the two largest sample sizes and development score differences remain within 1% of the common baseline. This is a sensitivity check, not an increase in effective sample size. If unstable, report the admissible capacity range and obtain more independent evidence.

**Pilot, subject to approval.** First run read-only shadow mode and reconcile telemetry and bills. Then use opt-in V1G on compatible infrastructure under operator oversight, with local electrical limits, a kill switch, communication-loss reversion and a human rollback procedure. Compare standard operation with the proposed controller using randomized weekday-matched blocks, or predeclare an alternative crossover if randomization is infeasible. Use a fixed enrollment and assignment protocol, track interruptions and early departures, preserve the applicable running peak, and document inter-block carryover. Shadow schedules cannot establish an observed causal effect; cumulative billed monthly maxima span multiple blocks and must not be attributed to one day's controller. Aim for 8–12 weeks for planning only; determine the number of independent randomized blocks from a blinded variance estimate and available operations. Existing hardware suffices to test implementation and service; it does not validate savings from uninstalled PV or BESS.

**External validation.** [ACN-Data](https://ev.caltech.edu/dataset) offers observed EV sessions for external timing and charging-feasibility tests. Check field availability and time-zone handling; observed delivery cannot replace missing requested energy. [Building Data Genome 2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-00712-x) is hourly measured institutional/building chronology and is suitable for separate hourly tests, not validation of a 15-minute demand charge. [CU-BEMS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-020-00582-3) is a measured Thai commercial-building dataset, not a KKU bill or an EV dataset. Do not splice unrelated load and session records and call the combination a measured integrated site. If replay tests combine independently measured sources, label the integration **hybrid scenario replay**, and do not use it as the sole proof of integrated field performance. For route B+D, qualify external sites using aligned meter load and relevant service evidence, adequate resolution and independent test periods. If these cannot be secured, do not claim multi-site validation.

**Statistics and power.** Pair replay at the **site × billing-cycle** level, aggregate shared calendar shocks before claiming independent evidence, and use moving blocks or cluster-aware uncertainty where the sample supports them. The primary cost outcome is a normalized paired difference; where lifecycle costs use operating-cost annualization, define the same extrapolation for each arm and allocate deterministic costs consistently across paired periods. In the field, use assignment block as the unit of inference and report sessions as nested, not independent randomized units. Estimate variance and dependence on development or blinded pilot data, and plan for 80% power at the chosen effect margin. For a rough independent-pair calculation against zero at two-sided 5%, $n\approx[(1.96+0.84)\sigma_d/\delta]^2$; use block or randomization simulation for the final design and power relative to the actual decision margin. Report sample counts and confidence intervals. Without adequate independent periods, report descriptive stress outcomes and do not claim a demonstrated tail benefit or service non-inferiority.

## 6. Secondary analyses and their limits

- **Meter pooling:** Where at least two independently billed points exist, predeclare two ways to map pooled capacities back to feasible locations: (1) pro rata to verified annual eligible load with documented technical caps and a fixed residual allocation order, and (2) a meter-aware placement solve on development data with pooled total capacity fixed. Re-evaluate both with physical billing, service and forecasting. If no feasible placement preserves the pooled totals, record infeasibility; resizing is a separate sensitivity. Keep tariff harmonization and actual-account outcomes separate. The basic peak coincidence inequality is a sanity check, not novelty.
- **Legacy comparators:** P0 = deterministic joint design from a point-estimate trajectory; P_RN = scenario-based expected-cost design; P1 = scenario-based expected-cost/CVaR design with the same causal information assumptions as P_RN. Optional defaults: confidence $\alpha=0.90$ and tail weight $\lambda=0.25$, with strictly positive mean weight ($0<\lambda<1$) when used. With insufficient data, leave CVaR as sensitivity only. Dsep/D1 and Psep/P0 are descriptive context, not evidence of scientific contribution merely because one feasible set contains another.
- **Carbon:** Report electricity-related Scope 2 separately with a documented boundary, factor and vintage. Do not infer avoided emissions, project credits or campus Net Zero from tariff savings. A fixed-factor import cap does not constitute a separate research result.
